Servo Press QC Guide

One setup method for every press

ShaftFlux ringBearing

The press can't tell you a joint is good, so we teach it to. Once a part is pressed you can't see or measure the joint. Setup turns the press's own end position into a pass/fail on every part.

It catches the two real failures: pressed short (not seated) and pressed too hard (damaged). Three presses build the motor: 1shaft2flux ring3bearings

The method, in order

1Gauge check
1.000 mm

Read a gauge block, ±0.003. Bearing stack mic: 26.000.

Why: every number after depends on it.

2Ladder
12 parts × 5 levels

Press and measure at 30% to 150% of today's pressure.

Why: finds where parts stop moving. That sets production pressure.

3Confirm
15 fresh parts

Press at production pressure, then again at the top level.

Why: a part that moves >0.01 mm means pressure is too low.

4Short parts
3 on purpose

Press 0.10, 0.20, 0.30 mm short of seated.

Why: known-bad parts to prove the limit rejects.

5Min and max
30 run parts

Window = run average ± 4σ. Min is raised if it would pass a short part.

Why: the limit the press enforces.

6Prove it
NG on bad parts

Shorts must fail. 0.20 mm shim test. 30 parts, no NG. Cpk ≥ 1.33.

Why: untested limits aren't trusted.

Step 2: the ladder finds "seated"

Each line is one part. Green rings mark where it stopped moving. Production pressure goes just past the tightest part. Illustrative.

Steps 4 to 6: the window catches short parts

If ±4σ would let the closest short part in, min is raised to halfway between it and the run average. Illustrative.

When something fails

  • Still movingRaise the top level if damage allows. Repeat.
  • Confirm part movedRaise pressure one step. Repeat with 10+ fresh parts.
  • Marks or damageStop. Pressure is too high.
  • Bad part passedWindow is wrong. Redo step 5.
  • Cpk < 1.33Too much scatter. Fix it or the measurement.
